About Anthony

Rooted on the Northside

Anthony J. Brown is a Jacksonville community advocate and the founder of Red Alliance for Justice. The group meets every third Thursday in New Town and works to repair neighborhoods shaped by redlining, the 1968 consolidation, and decades of local policy.

He served as co-chair of the Economic Committee and on the executive board of the NAACP Jacksonville Branch. Through his channel Tony's Tangent and the panel podcast Political Bombcast, he has walked viewers through redlining maps, blighted blocks, and empty grocery corridors. He has also talked through all of it with neighbors, pastors, educators, and state lawmakers.

His argument is simple. The neighborhoods that were mapped out of investment eighty years ago are still waiting on basics: lights, crosswalks, sewer lines, a grocery store, a fair share of the budget. Fixing that is how the whole city gets stronger.

Neighborhood spotlight

Durkeeville: the Miracle on Myrtle

In June 2025 the city finished a revitalization plan for historic Durkeeville, the neighborhood around Myrtle Avenue and Kings Road beside Edward Waters University. It lists more than 40 projects in three phases. Anthony's question is the one residents have asked since a 1996 federal grant stalled out: will the people who live here now be the ones who benefit?

“Withintrification”

The city's plan uses the word. Anthony and his allies were saying it in 2021, when he joined a panel called Withintrification and the Color of Law. It means revitalization led by the people already living here: homeownership, Black-owned storefronts on Myrtle, and no pricing out the families who held the neighborhood together.

How we got here

Ninety years, one map

The lines drawn in the 1930s still predict which Jacksonville streets flood, which lack sewer, and which wait longest for a grocery store.

JacksonvilleAnthony's work
  1. 1937
    Redlining maps drawn.

    Federal appraisers grade Black neighborhoods like Durkeeville, New Town, and the Eastside “hazardous,” cutting them off from loans for generations.

  2. 1968
    City–county consolidation.

    Residents are promised sewer service and other services. About 35 neighborhoods, many of them Black, are still on septic tanks decades later.

  3. 1996
    $21.5M HUD grant for Durkeeville stalls.

    Developers don't use the tax credits, and contamination from an old city incinerator stops construction.

  4. 2006
    Anthony starts organizing.

    The beginning of nearly twenty years of community advocacy on the Northside.

  5. 2020
    Hemming Park's Confederate monument comes down.

    The park is renamed for James Weldon Johnson, sixty years after Ax Handle Saturday.

  6. 2021
    “Redlining in Jacksonville.”

    On Tony's Tangent, Anthony lays the old maps over today's neighborhoods. He joins urban planner Ennis Davis for “Redlining… Remedies… Opportunities.”

  7. 2022
    Fighting the new district lines.

    As NAACP economic committee co-chair, he speaks out against the city's redistricting map. Red Alliance for Justice holds its first town hall.

  8. 2024
    Durkeeville study signed.

    The mayor signs $200K for a neighborhood revitalization plan.

  9. 2025
    The morgue, and a Bill of Rights.

    Red Alliance demands answers on the Medical Examiner's office built on a residential Brentwood street. The group pushes the city to codify a Neighborhood Bill of Rights for New Town, Sugar Hill, and the Northside.

  10. 2026
    Transit crisis.

    JTA faces a $39M gap. Up to 194 layoffs, Skyway passenger service ending, weekday bus cuts.

  11. 2027
    Jacksonville votes.

    Mayor, Sheriff, and all 19 City Council seats on the March 9 ballot.
